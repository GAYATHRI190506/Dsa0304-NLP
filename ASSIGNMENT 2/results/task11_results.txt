5. STRENGTHS AND WEAKNESSES
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLE Bigram:
  Strength: Simple and easy to calculate.
  Weakness : High sparsity and zero-probability problem.

Add-k:
  Strength: Simple smoothing and removes zero probabilities.
  Weakness : Performance depends on the selected k value.

Good-Turing:
  Strength: Adjusts probabilities using frequency-of-frequency information.
  Weakness : More complicated than Add-k.

Katz Backoff:
  Strength: Uses lower-order models when higher-order evidence is unavailable.
  Weakness : More implementation complexity.

Stupid Backoff:
  Strength: Lowest measured perplexity and simple implementation.
  Weakness : Uses a fixed backoff factor rather than a normalized probability model.

Rule-Based Tagger:
  Strength: Very fast, interpretable and achieved 100% test accuracy on this corpus.
  Weakness : Requires manually designed lexical dictionaries and rules.

HMM Viterbi:
  Strength: Models dependencies between neighbouring tags.
  Weakness : Slower than the rule-based approach and requires statistical training.

Brill Tagger:
  Strength: Learns transformations that correct initial tagging errors.
  Weakness : Training is slower than the rule-based tagger and the initial tagger was already highly accurate on this corpus.

6. FINAL RECOMMENDATION
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Recommended language model: Stupid Backoff
Measured perplexity: 3.1869
It achieved the lowest perplexity among the Task 4 models.

Recommended POS tagger for this public-transport corpus: Rule-Based Tagger
Measured test accuracy: 100.00%
Measured runtime: 0.1031 seconds
It provides the best combination of measured accuracy, speed and interpretability for this controlled corpus.

HMM Viterbi is a useful alternative when sequence-based statistical modelling is preferred.
Brill is useful as a transformation-based correction approach, but its improvement was small because the initial tagger was already highly accurate.

7. LIMITATION
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
The corpus is synthetic and highly regular. Therefore, the measured 100% rule-based and Brill accuracy should not be interpreted as evidence that the same performance will occur on unrestricted real-world speech.
The Day-4 challenge in Task 12 is used to test robustness under more difficult conditions such as unseen vocabulary and code-mixed speech.
